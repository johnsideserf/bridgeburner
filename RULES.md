# Bridgeburner

*A two-player card game of building & sabotage.*
2 players · standard 52-card deck · ~15 minutes per round

> Plain-text version of `Bridgeburner_Rules.pdf` (generated by
> `make_rules.py`). Keep the two in sync when rules change.
> Ruleset locked 2026-08-27 after simulation (see README): **the pile is the
> clock, torches within 2 ranks, equal turns.**

## Setup

Shuffle the deck. Deal **7 cards** to each player. Flip **3 cards face-up** in
the middle: this shared pool is the **River** (always 3 face-up cards). The
rest becomes a face-down **draw pile** — and the draw pile is the **clock**.
It is never reshuffled. When it runs out, the round ends.

## How to win

Build a **bridge**: a face-up row of **5 cards in ascending value** in front
of you. Suits don't matter and gaps are fine — each card just has to beat the
one before it. **Aces are low.**

- **Equal turns:** if the first player finishes a bridge, the second player
  gets one last turn.
- **The clock:** when the draw pile runs out, the round ends after that turn —
  the longer bridge wins.
- **Ties** (both finished, or equal length): compare the top card, then the
  next card down, and so on. Identical bridges are a drawn round.

Play best of three.

### Face cards are mortar

A Jack or Queen at the end of your bridge is tough: burning it costs your
rival their entire turn (2 actions) instead of 1. Nothing burns a King — but
nothing builds past a King either. Overreach with mortar too early and you'll
have to Demolish your own work to keep climbing.

## On your turn: spend 2 actions

Mix and match, or repeat the same action twice — whatever the cost allows.

| Action | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Draw** | 1 | Take the top card of the draw pile into your hand. |
| **Build** | 2 | Play a card from your hand onto the right end of your bridge. It must be **higher** than your current rightmost card. Slow, deliberate work — a full build takes your whole turn, so your rival always gets a chance to answer. |
| **Burn** | 1\* | Discard a hand card that matches the **color** (red/black) of your opponent's rightmost bridge card, **beats it, and is at most 2 ranks above it**. A King can only burn a Jack or Queen; nothing burns a King. Their card is destroyed — and washes into the River, replacing its oldest card. |
| **Ford** | 1 | Discard any card from your hand, take any one of the 3 River cards, then refill the River from the draw pile. A known card beats a blind draw. |
| **Flush** | 1 | Discard all 3 River cards and deal 3 fresh ones from the draw pile. Fish for what you need — but your rival sees the new cards too, and the clock ticks 3. |
| **Demolish** | 1 | Remove the rightmost card of **your own** bridge to the discard pile. Sometimes you tear down a low card to rebuild a taller ascent. |

\* Mortar resists the flames: burning a Jack or Queen costs **both** actions.

## Optional house rules

Tested in simulation but not part of the standard game. Agree before dealing.

- **Quick foundations:** your first two bridge cards cost **1 action** each
  instead of 2 (the third card onward costs 2 as normal). Rounds run about
  a third shorter, bridges go up from turn 2, and comebacks are more common —
  at the cost of fewer burns per round.

## Arsonist's advice

- Every burn spent is a card not built — attack when it costs them more than
  it costs you.
- Torches must be close. High cards finish bridges; the cards just above your
  rival's cap burn them.
- Watch the colors: cap your bridge with a card whose next two ranks in that
  color are already gone.
- The River remembers. Burned cards resurface there — don't hand your rival
  their card back.
- The pile is the clock. Every draw and ford brings the end nearer — a
  half-built bridge beats an empty one.
- Mortar buys time, not immunity. A face-card cap costs your rival a full turn
  to burn — build while they swing.

*Five-card bridge wins · equal turns · best of three rounds takes the match.*
